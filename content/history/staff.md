The hospital's staff included surgeons, psychiatrists, Army Nurse Corps officers, technicians, administrators, and support personnel. It provided surgery, psychiatric triage, and stabilized patients for long-term evacuation to Japan.

One of the most well-documented operational aspects of the 121st was its role as a centralized hub for military psychiatry. During the war, the U.S. military realized that traditional psychiatric evacuation back to the United States resulted in permanent psychological casualties. Instead, psychiatrists stationed at the 121st pioneered a paradigm shift, treating what was then termed "gross stress reaction" (the historical predecessor to PTSD). Doctors and nurses documented treating men presenting with:

+ Severely impaired concentration
+ Acute memory loss
+ Clinical depression 
+ Intense social alienation
